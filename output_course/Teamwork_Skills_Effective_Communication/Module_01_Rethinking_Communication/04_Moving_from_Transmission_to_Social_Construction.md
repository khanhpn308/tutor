# Moving from Transmission to Social Construction

0:06/3:45

1x

## What if You Considered Communication Like an "Operating System?"

When we work in groups, communication isn’t just one skill among many—it’s the foundation of everything we do together. But here’s the problem: most of us operate with a very basic **“factory-installed” understanding of communication** , what scholars call **the transmission model**. 

  * This model treats communication as the neutral exchange of information between senders and receivers. 

  * It works fine when you’re giving directions or passing along a message, but it quickly breaks down when things get complicated. That’s because the transmission model assumes that meaning is fixed, objective, and easy to transfer—when in reality, communication is rarely that simple. 

  * This is why so many of our so-called “communication problems” are not about poor message clarity, but about deeper issues of meaning, understanding, and interpretation.  




## Is Your Operating System "Factory-Installed" or "Upgraded?"

Think about it like this… Imagine your communication theory as an operating system. The transmission model is like an older, factory-installed OS: it runs simple programs well, but struggles with newer, more complex ones. That’s why we need an upgrade. **The social construction model of communication is that upgrade.**

Instead of thinking of communication as just moving information from point A to point B, this perspective says communication is how we actually _make_  our social worlds with other people. It’s how we build meaning, negotiate understanding, and interpret each other’s actions. In other words, it’s less about sending and receiving messages, and more about co-creating the realities that shape our group life. Communication is not just about “the content” of our words, but also about “the relationship” they construct—what those words do, and how they shape our shared future. This is where “the big three” come in: meaning, understanding, and interpretation. That’s what a social construction model is all about. We’re moving from simple to sophisticated.  

## The Bottom Line?

If you only think of communication as transmission, you’ll miss the heart of what makes group work succeed or fail. Yes, good message clarity is important, but it’s not enough. What really matters is how your communication creates meaning and builds relationships that allow people with different backgrounds, perspectives, and goals to move forward together. Communication is not just the tool we use to exchange information—it’s the process through which we construct our shared reality.

## Ask Yourself This!

Think back to a time when you were part of a group and something went wrong. Maybe you felt misunderstood, or maybe a conflict escalated even though everyone seemed to understand each other’s words clearly. Ask yourself: 

  * Was this a transmission problem (information wasn’t sent, received, or understood correctly) or was it really a social construction problem (different interpretations of what the words meant, or different realities being created in the interaction)? 

  * How might thinking in terms of meaning, understanding, and interpretation have helped you navigate the situation differently?
